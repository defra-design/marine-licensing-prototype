Tell us step by step how you'll carry out this activity. This is sometimes called a methodology.

We use it to assess the activity's likely impacts, for example on navigation, wildlife, habitats and water quality. If we grant a licence, your description sets the limits of what you're allowed to do.

Only describe this activity, not your wider project. We'll ask about timings on the next pages.

What to include

  • Method: the order of works and any specific techniques, for example vibro piling
  • Equipment: what type, how many, the maximum size and how it will be used, for example ‘up to 2 excavators of no more than 20 tonnes’
  • Materials: what type, the maximum quantity, and what will happen to any waste or removed material
  • Getting to the site: how people, equipment and materials will get there, for example by road or by sea, and which harbour any vessels will come from. If you're only using existing roads, access points or shipping routes, you just need to say so. Give more detail if you need a new or temporary access route, or if your route passes through or next to a protected or sensitive area.
  • Size of the works: the maximum dimensions, and the total area you'll use at any one time, including equipment, storage and temporary works
  • Temporary storage and works: what, where, how big and for how long, and whether any of it will be below Mean High Water Springs (the average level the sea reaches at its highest tides)
  • When you've finished: how you'll remove equipment, materials and temporary works, and restore the site

If some details are not confirmed yet

For anything you're not sure about, either:

  • give the maximum, for example ‘no more than 200 tonnes of armour stone’
  • name the alternatives, for example ‘vibro piling, or impact piling if ground conditions do not allow it’

We'll assess the maximum, or every alternative you give us.

Do not use words like ‘approximately’, ‘where practicable’ or ‘may be required’ without giving a limit. We cannot assess them.
